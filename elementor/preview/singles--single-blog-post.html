AI search hasn’t replaced Google. It sits on top of it. And it rewards the same thing good local marketing always has: a clear, consistent, trustworthy presence everywhere customers look.

The short version

AI tools pull from your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews and the directories that mention you. Make all four accurate, specific and consistent, and you become the easy answer.

What “AI search” actually means for a local business

When someone asks an AI assistant a question, it doesn’t search the way you do. It gathers what it can find about businesses that match, then summarizes. Three things decide whether you’re in that summary:

  • Can it find you? Your name, address and phone number need to match everywhere.
  • Does it understand you? Your website has to say plainly what you do, where and for whom.
  • Does it trust you? Recent, detailed reviews are the strongest signal you control.

Start with your Google Business Profile

Your profile is the single most-read source about your business. Fill in every field, choose the most specific category, add your services with descriptions, and post at least twice a month.

Write pages that answer real questions

AI tools quote pages that answer a question directly. One page per service, with a clear first paragraph, prices or ranges where you can, and the towns you serve, beats one long “Services” page every time.

“Write for the customer’s question, not for the algorithm. The algorithm is trying to find the best answer to that question.”Chris Mitchell

Make reviews do the talking

Assistants lean on reviews to decide who’s “best.” Ask every happy customer, make it a two-tap link, and reply to every review. Specific reviews (“they fixed our furnace the same day in Exeter”) help more than five stars alone.

A 30-day checklist

  1. Audit your name, address and phone on Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and Facebook.
  2. Complete every field of your Google Business Profile.
  3. Rewrite your homepage’s first paragraph to say what you do, where and for whom.
  4. Create one page for each core service.
  5. Set up an automatic review request after every job.

Where this fits in the FirstSearch Framework

Showing up in AI search is part of FirstThought: getting found wherever customers look. It only works if FirstBrand is solid first, because AI can only repeat what your brand clearly says.
